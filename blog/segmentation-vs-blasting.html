Segmentation vs. Blasting: A Real A/B Test With $50K in Revenue at Stake

June 18, 2026 · 10 min read · By Ahmad Hasan

Most email marketers fall into one of two camps. Camp One blasts the same email to the entire list. Camp Two segments into a dozen micro-audiences. Both think they're right.

So I ran a head-to-head test with real money on the line.

The Setup

Client: mid-market eCom brand, 85,000 subscribers. We took their biggest promo of the quarter — a sitewide 25% off sale — and split the list:

  • Control (42,500): One email, same subject line, same creative, sent to everyone
  • Test (42,500): Same creative, but split into 4 segments with tailored subject lines, preview text, and product picks

The Four Segments

  1. VIPs (spent $200+ in last 90 days): Subject: "Exclusive: 25% off everything (just for you)" — Product picks: premium tier only
  2. Recent Buyers (purchased in last 30 days): Subject: "You just bought X — here's what pairs perfectly" — Product picks: cross-sells
  3. At-Risk (no purchase in 60+ days): Subject: "We miss you — here's 25% off to come back" — Product picks: bestsellers
  4. Window Shoppers (browsed, never bought): Subject: "Your wishlist is on sale" — Product picks: items from their browse history

What This Means

Segmentation added $23,300 in direct revenue from a single send. The effort was minimal — same creative, just tailored the subject lines, preview text, and product picks per segment. Took an extra 45 minutes of setup.

The unsub reduction is the hidden win. At-risk customers who got the generic blast unsubscribed at 4x the rate. Segmentation protected the list while driving more revenue.

Start With Two

You don't need four segments. Start with two: recent buyers vs. everyone else. Even that simple split will outperform a blast every time.

Email marketing is not about sending more — it's about sending better.
